Normal Delivery vs C-Section: Essential Antenatal Care & Nutrition Guide

Medical Author: Consultant Gynaecologist & Obstetrician
Published: 2026 5 min read

Compassionate Maternity & Antenatal Healthcare

Authored by senior obstetricians at S.K Multi-Speciality Hospital to guide expectant mothers through safe trimesters, regular ultrasound scans, nutritional balancing, and birthing choices.

1. Aiming for a Safe Normal Vaginal Delivery

Normal vaginal delivery is the most natural process of childbirth, associated with faster postpartum recovery, lower infection risks, and early initiation of breastfeeding. At S.K Hospital, our obstetric team prioritizes normal delivery whenever clinically safe for both the mother and baby.

2. When is a Caesarean Section (C-Section) Recommended?

While normal delivery is always encouraged, certain medical circumstances make a planned or emergency C-section essential to ensure maternal and fetal safety:

  • Fetal Distress: Sudden drop in baby's heart rate during labor.
  • Breech Presentation: Feet or buttocks facing downward instead of head.
  • Placenta Previa: Placenta partially or completely blocking the cervix.
  • Cephalopelvic Disproportion (CPD): Baby's head size not fitting the pelvic opening.
  • Previous Multiple C-Sections or Severe Pre-eclampsia: Uncontrolled high maternal blood pressure.

3. Trimester-Wise Nutrition & Antenatal Checkups

Proper nutrition during pregnancy fuels fetal brain and organ development:

  1. First Trimester (1-12 Weeks): Mandatory Folic Acid supplementation to prevent neural tube defects, baseline blood group, Rh factor, Hb, and dating ultrasound scan.
  2. Second Trimester (13-28 Weeks): Iron and Calcium supplements, Anomaly Ultrasound Scan (TIFFA) to evaluate fetal anatomy, and screening for Gestational Diabetes.
  3. Third Trimester (29-40 Weeks): Regular fetal Doppler scans, non-stress tests (NST), monitoring baby kicks, and preparing the birth plan.
